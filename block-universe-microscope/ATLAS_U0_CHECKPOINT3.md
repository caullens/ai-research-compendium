Multiscale state-neighbor seeding plus analytic-Jacobian pair root solves has now produced **six distinct exact transverse double fibers**. Each solves F(a)=F(b) to ~1e-13 or better, has a != b, and the 8×8 combined derivative [J(a), -J(b)] has rank 8.

*Sign is computed from det([J(a), -J(b)]) after choosing the current orientations. The existence of the crossing is orientation-free; the sign needs orientation structure. Because the source dimension is even, swapping the two sheets does not change the sign.

The six currently known signs are three positive and three negative, so their signed sum is zero. This is suggestive of a global self-intersection cancellation law but is **not yet evidence that the census is complete**; additional +/- pairs could exist farther toward infinity.

For each of the six shared states, independent inverse solves from many unrelated starts recovered exactly the two known preimages and no third preimage. This is consistent with the generic expectation that triple points should be absent for a 4→8 self-transverse immersion.

### First-layer asymptotic sector structure (microscope/architecture level)

The 24 first-layer row vectors define a central hyperplane arrangement in R⁴. Numerically, every tested 4-row subset is independent, so the arrangement is in general position. Its top-dimensional chambers number

2 * sum_{k=0}^{3} C(23,k) = 4096.

All 4096 chamber sign patterns were enumerated exactly by visiting neighborhoods of triple-hyperplane intersections. Generic rays in one chamber approach the same first-layer sign state, and thus one finite output limit. The sampled/output limit states are distinct numerically.

Important prior audit: the **4096-sector decomposition is not yet claimed as black-box ontology**. It uses the known first-layer architecture. What *is* intrinsic is that U₀ has a nontrivial cluster set at infinity. The hyperplane/zonotope analysis is an external microscope for understanding that cluster set.

## Zonotope compactification microscope

Define

φ(x) = Σ_i log cosh(w_i·x + b_i)

using the 24 first-layer normals w_i. Then

∇φ(x) = W₁ᵀ tanh(W₁x+b₁).

Its Hessian is

W₁ᵀ diag(sech²(W₁x+b₁)) W₁,

which is positive definite at every finite x because W₁ has rank 4 and the diagonal entries are positive. Thus this map is a strict-convexity coordinate on the source.

The recession/support function is that of the zonotope

Z = Σ_i [-w_i, w_i].

This gives a useful architecture-aware compactification chart: R⁴ is represented as the interior of a bounded 4D zonotope and escape to infinity approaches its boundary. Numerical inversion succeeded on all 80 randomly chosen interior zonotope targets tested, to residual <= 2e-12.

For the generic 24-hyperplane arrangement, the associated 4D zonotope has the following boundary face counts:

- vertices: 4096
- edges: 12192
- 2-faces: 12144
- 3-faces: 4048

and 4096 - 12192 + 12144 - 4048 = 0, as expected for an S³ boundary cell decomposition.

Again: this zonotope is currently an **external global chart**, not something the future ontology learner is handed.

## Major conceptual result of this checkpoint

The full-diffeomorphism gauge is extremely strong. At regular points it erases every local metric/visual property. Consequently the strict ontology of a bare smooth map is driven primarily by:

- differential rank strata;
- fiber multiplicity;
- transverse multiple points;
- quotient/global topology;
- properness and behavior at infinity;
- global incidence/knotting data.

This means a frozen random neural field can look extraordinarily rich while having a surprisingly sparse strict local ontology. Conversely, the isolated self-identifications and non-proper infinity structure are real features that cannot be dismissed as plotting artifacts.
